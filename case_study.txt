# Case Study: Project Meridian – Modernizing Legacy Core Banking for 10x Transactional Throughput

### Executive Summary
A mid-sized regional bank faced a critical ceiling: their legacy core banking system, built on monolithic architecture, could not handle more than 50 transactions per second (TPS). As they prepared to launch a new mobile-first digital brand, they needed to reach 500 TPS without a multi-year "rip and replace" project. Regent engineered a high-throughput integration layer that decoupled the digital front-end from the legacy core, achieving 10x throughput in 6 months.

---

### The Context
"Meridian Bank" (pseudonym) has served the region for 40 years. Their reputation is built on stability. However, their core banking system—the "Single Source of Truth"—was a 15-year-old monolithic application that processed transactions sequentially.

While sufficient for traditional branch banking, the core was unprepared for the "Mobile Surge"—the thousands of concurrent requests generated by a modern mobile app (checking balances, small P2P transfers, real-time notifications).

---

### The Problem: The Sequential Bottleneck
Meridian’s core system used "Pessimistic Locking." When a transaction was processed, the entire account record was locked until completion. At 50 TPS, the lock contention was so high that new requests would time out, leading to app crashes and customer frustration.

**The Challenges:**
1. **Concurrency Cap:** The system hit a hard physical limit at 50 TPS.
2. **Data Silos:** The mobile app team couldn't access real-time data without hammering the already-struggling core.
3. **Risk of Failure:** A full "core migration" was estimated at $15M and 3 years—too slow and too risky.

---

### The Approach: The "Sidecar Core" Strategy
Regent proposed a "Sidecar" architecture. Instead of replacing the legacy core, we built a high-performance **Read-Optimized Shadow Core** using Regent Data.

**Key Steps:**
1. **CDC (Change Data Capture):** We implemented a non-invasive listener on the legacy core’s database. Every time a transaction occurred, it was emitted as an event.
2. **Event-Driven Projection:** These events were streamed into a modern, distributed database designed for massive concurrency. This "Shadow Core" served 99% of the mobile app's requests (balance checks, history).
3. **Asynchronous Command Pattern:** For writes (transfers), the mobile app sent requests to a "Command Queue." Regent Integrate managed the throttled execution of these commands against the legacy core, ensuring it was never overwhelmed.

---

### The Execution
The project was delivered in three phases:
*   **Month 1-2:** Infrastructure setup and CDC implementation. Verification that the "Shadow Core" was 100% consistent with the legacy core.
*   **Month 3-4:** Migration of "Read" traffic. The mobile app was updated to query the Shadow Core instead of the legacy system. API latency dropped from 800ms to 45ms.
*   **Month 5-6:** Implementation of the "Command Queue" for writes and the rollout of the new digital brand.

---

### The Results (Quantified)
By the end of the 6-month engagement, Meridian Bank achieved:
*   **Throughput:** Increased from 50 TPS to a sustained **550 TPS** (11x increase).
*   **Latency:** P99 API latency reduced by **94%**.
*   **Availability:** The digital brand maintained 100% uptime, even during "Black Friday" surges where the legacy core experienced brief slowdowns.
*   **Cost Efficiency:** The project cost less than 15% of a full core replacement while delivering the same business value.

---

### Key Lessons
1. **Don't Fight the Core:** You don't always need to replace legacy systems; you often just need to decouple from them.
2. **Reads are 90% of the Battle:** Most load on a banking system is from people checking their balance. Solving for "Reads" via projections removes 90% of the pressure on the "Write" core.
3. **Events are the Universal Language:** By treating every transaction as an event, Meridian gained the ability to plug in new services (AI fraud detection, real-time analytics) without ever touching the legacy code again.
